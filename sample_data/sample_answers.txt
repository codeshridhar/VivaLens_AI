QUESTION: What is A* search and why is it better than uniform cost search?

ANSWER:
A* search is an informed search algorithm that selects the next node to
expand using the evaluation function f(n) = g(n) + h(n), where g(n) is the
cost already paid to reach the node and h(n) is the heuristic estimate of
the remaining cost to reach the goal. It is better than uniform cost search
because the heuristic guides the search towards the goal, so it expands far
fewer nodes. With an admissible heuristic, A* is also guaranteed to return
an optimal path.

QUESTION: Explain forward chaining and backward chaining.

ANSWER:
Forward chaining starts from the known facts in the knowledge base and
repeatedly applies the rules whose premises are satisfied, deriving new
facts until no further rule can fire. It is fast and productive but may
generate many irrelevant facts. Backward chaining works in the opposite
direction: it starts from the goal, selects a rule that could produce it,
and then recursively tries to satisfy the premises of that rule. It is
useful when the goal is specific and the search space is large.

QUESTION: What is a deadlock and what are the four necessary conditions?

ANSWER:
A deadlock is a situation in which a set of processes are each waiting for
an event that only another process in the same set can cause, so none of
them can proceed. Coffman identified four necessary conditions: mutual
exclusion, hold and wait, no preemption, and circular wait. A deadlock can
only occur when all four hold simultaneously. Deadlock prevention breaks
one of the conditions directly, while deadlock avoidance uses safe-state
checks and the Banker's algorithm to choose allocations carefully.

QUESTION: What is normalisation and why are 1NF, 2NF and 3NF needed?

ANSWER:
Normalisation is the process of organising relations to reduce redundancy
and avoid update anomalies. First normal form requires atomic values and no
repeating groups. Second normal form additionally requires that every
non-prime attribute depends on the whole candidate key, which removes
partial dependency. Third normal form requires that every non-prime
attribute depends only on the key, removing transitive dependency.
Boyce-Codd normal form is the strongest of the practical normal forms.

QUESTION: How does the TCP three-way handshake establish a connection?

ANSWER:
The client sends a SYN segment with an initial sequence number. The server
replies with a SYN-ACK segment carrying its own initial sequence number and
acknowledging the client's number plus one. The client then sends an ACK
acknowledging the server's sequence number plus one. At this point both
sides agree on the initial sequence numbers and the connection is
established, which also synchronises both sequence number spaces.
